DEMASTER changelog (MCINDUS's continuation)

v15.13 (in the source; not packaged yet) - battles and the world map: day and night, the sun over the stage, stage lights
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built in two steps. The first (day and night, the rules, glows and AO) ran in the game: the log and six frame logs came
back and the second step is built from them. The second step has not run in the game yet. The rules and the shader are
tested on Linux (the shader against sums worked out on the CPU); the Windows-only files are type-checked.
Day and night in battles and on the world map (new: SCENE_LIGHT, on by default; DEMASTER_EXP\scene_light.txt)
  - Battles and the world map are drawn by the game's main.frag from textures times vertex colours. DEMASTER's main.frag
    now multiplies that picture by a colour, in linear light: nothing by day, night_level x night_tint by night, with
    the clock's fades in between. In battle: the stage and the fighters. On the world map: everything drawn from the
    world's own textures. Draws that add light (spells, glows) and the menus and text are left alone.
  - Where the night comes (night = auto): the world map always. A battle when it started on the world map, in a field
    open to the sky (sky = 1 in its _pbr.txt) or in a field that is always night (time = night). Battles started in
    other fields stay as painted, so indoor stages don't go dark. Battles keep their slower clock.
  - DEMASTER_EXP\scene_light.txt (written with the defaults the first time, read again when you save it): [battle] and
    [world] with night, time, day_tint, night_tint, night_level, ao, glow.
Rules for places and for the story (the Lunar Cry)
  - [rule <name>] in scene_light.txt changes any of those settings where its conditions hold: where = battle / world /
    both, stage = 12, 40-45 (battle stages by number), moment = 3000-65535 (the story's progress, the game's own
    counter, read from the game: EFIGS and JP). Later rules go over earlier ones.
  - The file comes with [rule lunar cry]: from counter 3000 on (the Ragnarok is yours) the night is red instead of blue
    and the day slightly warm, in battles and on the world map. The colours are a starting point: change or delete it.
    SCENE_DEBUG=1 writes the counter to the log whenever a battle or the world map starts.
Battle stages: a picture of the glows, and AO (the maps next to the stage page's picture in battle.fs\hd_new)
  - a0stgNNN_P_EC (.dds or .png): the glows as a picture, exactly as fields have them since v15.12 (colour times
    coverage, coverage in alpha): it takes the darkened stage's place by night. fieldprep doesn't make it for stages
    yet: paint it with an alpha of its own (a layer over the page), or on black.
  - a0stgNNN_P_ORM: an ORM map; its red channel (AO) darkens the page by "ao" of it. Roughness and metallic are not used
    yet.
  - An animated page's frames (a0stgNNN_P_F) use their own maps when they have them, else the page's.
Battle stages lit from their own space: the sun and moon overhead, normal maps, four lights a stage (second step)
  - From your frame logs and the registers the first step logged: the numbers the game projects with (the PlayStation's
    rotation, translation, screen centre and distance, kept in the game's memory) are the battle camera's when the game
    hands its draws to OpenGL. With them the corners of stage 101's sky, taken from the frame log of the same moment,
    come out as one cylinder 30,000 units around the stage's middle, and its ground as the world map's grid. So every
    pixel's place in the stage can be worked out in the shader (z is 1 / rhw), and the stage's own space stands still
    while the camera moves. That is now a test with those very numbers.
  - The sun and the moon cross the stage by the clock, as in fields (sun_azimuth, sun_height in [battle]); where the
    night doesn't come they stand at their highest. sun = how much a surface turned away from it darkens (the
    surface's direction is worked out per pixel from the screen; nothing is read from the stage's file).
  - a0stgNNN_P_N: a normal map for a stage page (tangent space, as for field models). relief = how strongly it shows in
    the sun's light. With an _ORM map the sun also leaves a highlight (specular; roughness and metallic are used now).
  - Up to four lights of a stage's own, in scene_light.txt: light = x up z  r g b  range [night|day] inside a
    [rule] with stage = N. They light the stage and the fighters (the fighters' surfaces come out faceted: they have no
    normals of their own here).
  - The camera is read once a frame. A frame whose registers are not a rotation, or that jump away for a single frame
    (something else was projected last), keeps the camera in use; a cut is taken on its second frame.
  - A battle on the world map stands on the world's own textures (types 18 and 27): they are lit as the stage. The
    world map's sky and haze now darken with the night too (in the first step the sky stayed bright).
What this is not (yet)
  - Seen in the game: the camera was checked against one frame with the camera at rest. Whether the registers are the
    camera's in every frame of a camera move, a spell or a summon is what the filter is there for; SCENE_DEBUG=1 still
    logs them at a stage draw.
  - Shadows: the fighters cast none on the stage from the sun or the lights.
  - Stage pages DEMASTER doesn't replace are dimmed and get the lights but not the sun's part. World map textures have
    no maps yet, and the world map itself only darkens and takes the night's colour.
For working on the code
  - scene_light.h/.cpp (the rules, the camera, its filter, the sun, the lights; field_pbr_test, 17,958 checks),
    tests/scene_shader_test.cpp (main.frag with DEMASTER's uniforms off, the night, AO, the glows, and a floor drawn
    as the game hands it over with that battle's camera: the sun's part, a light, a normal map; 24 checks).

v15.12 (in the source; not packaged yet) - glows as a picture, the turn swings, world-map stick, pages that stay pages
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not run in the game yet. The shared code and the shader are tested on Linux (the new glows against sums worked out on
the CPU, pixel for pixel); the Windows-only files are type-checked.
The glows as a picture (FIELD_PBR; new file <field>_EC, new setting day in [emissive])
  - Until now the glows were a mask (the alpha of _ORME) that lit up the painting's own colour at night, and [emissive]
    tint multiplied it. A window painted blue by day could not glow yellow: the tint mixed with the blue and the old colour
    showed around it. Now your <field>_E.png can be a picture of the glows themselves, in any colour. fieldprep orme
    packs it into <field>_EC (BC7): the colour, and in the alpha how much of the painting the glow takes the place of
    (coverage). At night: painting x (1 - coverage) + glow. A lit window replaces what is painted there; a soft halo
    only adds its light.
  - Two ways to paint it. On black, without alpha: bright parts take the painting's place, dim ones add to it
    (--emissive-cover <%>: the brightness that replaces entirely, default 50; nothing below a fifth of that). Or with an
    alpha of its own: a layer over the night painting, exactly as a paint program shows it.
  - A grey _E.png without alpha is still the old mask and works as before. So do fields without any _E (the mask from
    _RESM). hue, tint and strength in [emissive] apply to both kinds.
  - [emissive] day = 0 to 1 in _lights.txt: how much of the glows shows in daylight (0: none, as before; 1: all). At
    night they show in full whatever it says.
  - With a picture of the glows _ORME's alpha is written plain white and not read: BC7 then spends all its bits on AO,
    roughness and metallic. One more texture per page of a field that has glows (about 1 MB a page at 4x) and one more
    texture read per pixel there.
  - One layer's own: <field>_<pupu>_E.png gives <field>_<pupu>_EC.
A picture of an animation state takes its state's maps (FIELD_PBR)
  - bccent_1 has one camera and two states of the whole scene (sharp street, sharp planter). The pictures 00401011 to
    00401015 had no _ND or _ORME of their own and took the field's, which show the other state: blocks lit by the wrong
    normals. A picture of an animation state without an _ND, _ORME or _EC of its own now takes the one of its state's
    first picture (the same name ending in 0: 00401010), and only then the field's. The log names the pictures that do.
    Nothing to rename or copy. _LF is not touched (fieldprep already writes one per picture where it differs).
Pages that stay pages (FIELD_LAYERS)
  - In a frame log one page of bccent_1 still held DEMASTER's picture but was no longer on DEMASTER's list, so the game's
    own program drew it: unlit, no shadows, no night. Four places take a page off the list and none said so in the log.
  - The check that the game still uses a page's texture for that page asked the driver about "the bound texture" while
    going by the remembered binding for which page it was. It now asks only when the driver has that same texture bound,
    and leaves the page alone otherwise.
  - When a .map is read the list is emptied (another field's textures can reuse the numbers). Pages are now set aside
    instead: if the game then draws one without having given its texture a new picture, and it is still the same field,
    the page is taken back with its PBR maps. A new upload by the game forgets it as before.
  - FIELD_DEBUG: a line in the log every time a page leaves the list, with the reason, and every time one is taken back.
  - Which cause it was in that session is not known (its log was not there). The next log will say.
The party light follows the character you move (FIELD_MOVING_LIGHTS)
  - It went to "the playable character that moved last", and followers move after the leader stops. The game keeps the
    index of the script entity the pad moves (the field's movement code compares each entity with it before reading
    the stick); the light now goes to that one. [party] follow in _lights.txt still wins; if that entity is not drawn,
    the old rule applies. EFIGS and JP (FIELD_PLAYER_ENTITY in mymemory.h).
The turn swings (new: MOVE_TURN_SPEED, 720 degrees a second; 0 = off)
  - Fields: the d-pad and the arrow keys now walk through the game's analogue code too, as a stick pushed all the way in
    the direction held, and the direction given to the game turns towards it the short way round instead of jumping:
    a quarter turn takes an eighth of a second, a turn back a quarter. The left stick swings the same; how hard it is
    pushed is passed on unchanged. After 0.3 s with nothing held the next push starts in its own direction.
  - The buttons word is read from the movement code's own argument on the game's stack (the same place in EFIGS and JP).
  - To check in the game: walk and run with the d-pad still follow your walk/run button as before (the analogue code
    reads the same button, but that path has only been read, not played).
The world map: the left stick moves at any angle (new: ANALOG_WORLD_MAP, on by default; needs ANALOG_MOVEMENT)
  - The world map's input code asks for all four axes of both pads each frame (eight calls). With an XInput pad
    connected, pad 1's are answered: the left stick as it is, the right stick at rest (Remastered turns the camera by
    buttons). While the left stick is pushed, the d-pad bits are taken out of that frame's keys (Remastered also sends
    the stick as d-pad presses), as FFNx does for the 2000 game. Without a pad: as before. EFIGS and JP.
In-between pictures: off by default (FIELD_INTERPOLATION=0)
  - In the game it slowed fields down (38 to 56 ms a frame against 33 without) and its own check for that did not catch
    it. Shelved until it is rebuilt another way; the switch is still there.
For working on the code
  - tests: the glows' picture and its packing, [emissive] day, who carries the light when the game says who you move
    (field_pbr_test, 17,892 checks), the d-pad as a stick and the swing (field_tween_test, 605 checks), and the shader's
    new glows by night, tinted, and by day at day = 0.5 (pbr_shader_test, when FIELD's folder has an _EC.png).

v15.11 (in the source; not packaged yet) - walking at any angle, smooth scroll, in-between pictures (60 a second)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
None of this has run in the game yet: it was written against the game's code and a frame log, the rules and the
framebuffer copying are tested on Linux, and the Windows-only files are type-checked. Each part has its own switch.
Walking at any angle (new: ANALOG_MOVEMENT, on by default; ANALOG_DEADZONE 0.25)
  - Fields: the pad's left stick walks in its own direction, not the d-pad's eight. The 2000 game still has the
    PlayStation's analogue walking; it asks a function "is there a stick" three times a frame and Remastered always answers
    no. Those three calls now go through DEMASTER, which answers with an XInput pad's left stick while it is outside its
    dead zone (FFNx does the same for the 2000 game). Stick at rest, no pad, keyboard: exactly as before.
  - EFIGS and JP addresses (ANALOG_GET_VALUE and the three call sites in mymemory.h; both DLLs have the same 31 calls to
    that function, in the same order). Not on the world map: that also needs the d-pad bits taken out of the game's key
    state, and that address is not known.
Smooth scroll (new: FIELD_SMOOTH_SCROLL, on by default; fields drawn from layer images)
  - Every layer is drawn at the unrounded place of its scroll. The game places layers by whole-number arithmetic, so they
    move in steps of a PlayStation pixel, and layers at other speeds than the main one step at other moments. DEMASTER
    reads each layer's place from the tiles the game draws, runs the places through a filter (an alpha-beta filter,
    critically damped: fieldtween::Axis) and moves the draw by the difference through the projection matrix, in whole
    framebuffer pixels. On steady scrolls the steps come out 6 to 8 times more even than the game's.
  - The characters move with the main layer's difference, so they stay on the floor. They are drawn before the
    background, so they wait for the main layer's first draw; opaque layers the game draws before the main one are kept
    back and drawn right after the characters, in the game's order. Without PBR the characters are kept and drawn as the
    game draws them.
  - Never further than 3/4 of a PlayStation pixel from the game's place; exactly the game's place at rest; a move of more
    than 12 pixels in a frame is a cut and is taken as it is; a layer that reaches an edge of the picture is never moved
    off it.
In-between pictures (new: FIELD_INTERPOLATION, on by default; 0 turns it off; fields drawn from layer images)
  - Before each of the game's frames an in-between picture is shown: every layer halfway along its scroll, every
    character's vertices halfway between the last frame's and this one's (same texture and vertex count: vertex by vertex;
    otherwise the model moves as a whole). The game keeps its own 30 frames a second; nothing in its logic is touched.
  - How: everything the game draws in a field frame is drawn a second time into DEMASTER's copies of the game's
    framebuffers (field_mirror.cpp), with only the layers' and characters' places changed, and so are its clears, blits
    and copies; the game's own anti-aliasing and final draw run on the copies too. At SwapBuffers the in-between picture
    is shown, half a frame later the game's own.
  - It measures the game's rate per field and only runs at about 30 pictures a second. If the game runs slower with it
    than without (more than 12% for two seconds), it turns itself off for that field and says so in the log; after three
    such fields, for the session. FIELD_GL_TRACK now also runs without FIELD_PBR when either of these is on.
  - Not in-between yet: FIELD_PBR's character shadows and reflections (the frame's own in both pictures), the game's
    round shadows, effects that are neither tiles nor characters. About double the GPU work in fields, and a second copy
    of the game's framebuffers in video memory.
Fixed (found in the first log from the game)
  - The field shader did not compile on NVIDIA cards since v15.8: one line used clamp() on whole numbers, which GLSL 1.20
    doesn't have ("demaster_field.frag does not compile: 0(380) : error C7011" in the log). Mesa, which the tests draw
    with, lets it through. With that shader file in the game folder PBR was off: the field drawn from its paintings, the
    game's own characters and round shadows. So day and night in the shader (v15.8), the glow fix and roll-off and the
    look and moving lights (v15.10) have not run on an NVIDIA card before this. tests/run_tests.sh now also reads every
    shader with glslangValidator, which stops at the same line.
  - FIELD_GL_TRACK turned itself off at the logo in every session ("2D texture binding: remembered 0, the driver has 3").
    The game calls some OpenGL functions under two names: glActiveTexture and glActiveTextureARB, glBlendEquation and
    glBlendEquationEXT / glBlendEquationSeparateEXT, and framebuffers only as glBindFramebufferEXT. The tracker watched one
    name of each. It now watches both where the driver has them as separate entries (the log says which), and when it
    still finds a difference it lists every kind of difference, not only the first.
For working on the code
  - tests/field_tween_test.cpp (the filter, the layer a draw belongs to, the character tween, the pacing, the stick) and
    tests/mirror_test.cpp (the framebuffer copies on a frame built like the game's, pixel for pixel, under Mesa). Both run
    in tests/run_tests.sh. CODE_GUIDE.md has a section on all three features.

v15.10 (in the source; not packaged yet) - the look, moving lights, night glows fixed, light rolls off, a Release build
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The look (new: FIELD_PBR_LOOK in demaster.conf, 0 = off by default)
  - The maps can now change the painting itself, not only light that is added. A master dial and three dials under it,
    each times the master and times the field's own "look" in its _pbr.txt:
      FIELD_PBR_RELIGHT (0.5)     the painted light follows the normal map: brighter towards the lamps that are on, darker
                                  away from them; the field as a whole keeps its brightness (the scale that does that is
                                  worked out per frame from a grid of the room's normals: fieldpbr::RelightScale)
      FIELD_PBR_HIGHLIGHTS (0.5)  the painted lamps make highlights (GGX) without their diffuse light a second time
      FIELD_PBR_AO (0.5)          the AO map darkens the painting too
  - Per field in _pbr.txt (kept by fieldprep, read again live): look, look_relight, look_highlights, look_ao.
  - With every dial at 0 nothing changes: a field at rest is still its painting, exactly.
Moving lights (new module: FIELD_MOVING_LIGHTS, on by default)
  - The sun (FIELD_SUN): on fields with sky = 1 (or [sun] on = 1) it crosses the sky with the day/night clock, adds light,
    casts the characters' shadows, and with the relight dial moves the painted light from morning to evening; a faint
    moon at night. Per field: [sun] in _lights.txt (on, azimuth, height, intensity, shade, moon, fixed).
  - The party light (FIELD_PARTY_LIGHT: 0 off, 1 at night, 2 always): a warm light the party leader carries. Per field:
    [party] in _lights.txt (on, color, intensity, range, height, follow). The leader is worked out from the script
    entities' "playable character" byte and who moved last; the log names the entity; follow = <entity> overrides it.
    Untested in the game.
  - They take places among the field's 8 lights (the field's faintest step aside when all 8 are in use).
Speed
  - The field shader goes over the lights once per pixel instead of up to three times (painted light, night, added
    light); pixels nothing is added to skip the roll-off's arithmetic.
  - The field program's uniforms that are the same for a whole frame (all but 3 of them) are sent once per frame
    instead of before every background draw.
  - The look reads no extra textures; the moving lights cost what any light costs.
For working on the code
  - CODE_GUIDE.md: where everything is, how a frame goes through the modules, how to add a setting, a uniform or a light.
  - tools/syntax_check.sh: MinGW type-checks the DLL's Windows-only sources on Linux. tests/build_tests.sh and
    tests/run_tests.sh build and run fieldprep and every test from scratch.
Shaders
  - Night glows: the glow now takes the dimmed painting's place where the emissive map says (mix(dimmed painting, glow,
    emissive x night)). It was added on top of the dimmed painting, which already has the glow in it, so lamps came out
    1.1 to 1.9 times as bright as painted and burnt to white. As painted ([emissive] strength 1, tint 1 1 1, hue 0) a
    fully glowing pixel is now exactly the painting; strength and tint still change it.
  - Added light (the lights, the reflections) rolls off towards white instead of being cut off at it: painted P, added u,
    drawn P + (1 - P)(1 - exp(-u / (1 - P))) per channel. With nothing added the painting is exact, as before; a little
    added light looks as before; a light's intensity can go well above 1 without a flat white hole. Characters: the same
    over the room's light, for the added lights and the model's own glow.
Build
  - A Release build of ff8_demaster.dll (the solution's Release configuration; build_demaster.bat now makes it too, in
    .build\Release, after the Debug one and without touching it). Optimised, the release C++ runtime (no debug heap, no
    checked iterators), the log still written (DEMASTER_LOG). Until now only Debug builds existed.
  - vendor\lib\bgfx only has Debug builds of bx and bimg, which can't be linked into a release build, so Release compiles
    them from source: ff8_demaster\vendor\src (bx 0455026, bimg 5ee42f6: the revisions the headers in vendor\include are
    from, unchanged; README.txt there). The Debug build links the libraries as before.
  - The nine files with the game's hooks written in assembly (dllmain, iopatch, texture, the battle hooks, fieldBackground,
    fieldChara, worldVehicles) stay unoptimised in Release: some of their naked functions mix assembly with C++ and count
    on the compiler leaving the game's registers alone, which optimised code doesn't.
  - Not yet through Microsoft's compiler: the first Release build may need a fix (build_log_release.txt). Checked here:
    the bx/bimg sources compile and link for 32-bit Windows with MinGW and read our DDS and PNG files.
Tests

v15.8 - layer images from PuPu (its names and sizes); day and night on PBR fields
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Day and night (new: DAY_NIGHT in demaster.conf, on by default)
  - One clock for the whole game: 8 minutes of day and 4 of night, 40-second fades, in fields, on the world map and in
    menus; battles run it 3 times slower (24 + 12 minutes, 120-second fades) and the field goes on from where the battle
    left it. Kept in DEMASTER_EXP\daynight.txt between sessions. Lengths: DAY_NIGHT_DAY, _NIGHT, _FADE, _BATTLE.
  - PBR fields at night: time = day lights fade out and time = night lights fade in (each time of day keeps its own 2 of
    fieldprep's and 6 of yours; during a fade at most 8 at once, the faintest wait). The painted light dims to the lights
    file's [night] level x tint away from the lamps on at night; around those (night or both, baked) the painting keeps
    [night] lamps of its light within their range. The emissive glows come on. Characters lit by the room dim the same way.
  - A field's own time: time = cycle | day | night in its _pbr.txt (yours: fieldprep keeps it). day or night fixes it, and
    the clock waits while you're there (battles from it too), then goes on.
  - Testing: Ctrl+F7 swaps day and night, Ctrl+F8 pauses the clock, Ctrl+F9 runs it at 1x, 10x, 60x, Ctrl+F6 jumps to 5 s
    before the next fade (DAY_NIGHT_KEYS). A thin bar across the top shows the cycle and the clock while it's paused or
    sped up, and after a key (DAY_NIGHT_BAR). The log says what each key did and the time.
  - Not tied to game saves yet (loading an older save keeps the current time). Battles, the world map and non-PBR fields
    have no night look yet: they only move the clock.
Lights files
  - [night]: level (0.18), tint (0.6 0.7 1.0), lamps (0.9), for the whole field. Written by fieldprep, kept on --relight.
  - time = day | night now works: day and night lights cross-fade with the clock.
Shaders
  - demaster_field.frag and demaster_chara.frag: the night (demasterNight, demasterNightTint; demasterLightFlicker.w marks
    the night's lamps). Copy DEMASTER_EXP\shaders\ from this release.
DEMASTER and fieldprep
  - Read layer images exported by PuPu as well as FME's. The two tools name some layers differently (bdifrit1: FME's
    00CFF000/00C00000/00901000/01902000 are PuPu's 004FF000/00400000/00101000/01102000) and size them differently: FME
    makes every image the field's whole render area (bdifrit1 400x248), PuPu each layer the box around its tiles and the
    field's centre (bdifrit1: main and lava 336x240, 01102000 and 02103000 368x144). Per tile, the PuPu-named file at
    PuPu's size is used, else the FME-named file at FME's size. A folder can mix the two. PuPu's rule was checked
    against PuPuSharp's code on bdifrit1 and 300 random .map files.
  - A layer image whose size fits neither tool's frame (at a whole scale) is reported and not used; its tiles keep the
    game's pixels. Missing layers are listed by both names.
fieldprep
  - The field's canvas (the whole-field maps, the lights, the camera) is FME's render area when every image is FME's,
    else the box around the opaque layers' pictures: bdifrit1 from PuPu is 336x240 (1344x960 at 4x), so your maps
    are no longer resized to FME's 1600x992.
  - Maps made for the other frame (FME's canvas, or a layer picture's own) are cropped or padded into place by their
    exact offset, not stretched. Per-layer maps and outputs (<field>_<pupu>_A, _LF, ...) are in that layer's picture's
    frame, the same size as its layer image.
  - Writes canvas = left top width height into _pbr.txt. The report says how many images are PuPu's and FME's and which
    canvas it used.
  - After an earlier run on PuPu images: run with --rebake and --relight (the old _O and lights were on FME's canvas).
DEMASTER
  - Reads the canvas from _pbr.txt (FIELD_PBR on); without it, FME's render area, as before. The FIELD_DEBUG summary line

v15.3 (demaster.conf 1.5.2a) - depth fixed, faster, lights found by inverse lighting
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
New settings (demaster.conf, after FIELD_PBR_DEPTH):
  FIELD_PBR_QUALITY=medium  low (integrated graphics), medium (GTX 1060 class), high
  FIELD_GL_TRACK=1          the OpenGL state remembered as the game sets it, instead of asked of the driver around every draw

Depth (bvtr_3: the black band, the ticker across the screen, Zell seen through the door)
  - v15 wrote each background pixel's own depth (pushed back from _ND). Pushed-back tiles fell behind other tiles: the
    ceiling went behind the black backdrop and the ticker drawn after it, the door behind Zell. Now every tile keeps the
    game's own depth exactly, so tiles can't change places, and the GPU can skip hidden pixels again.
  - FIELD_PBR_DEPTH now works the other way round: DEMASTER copies the characters' depth right after drawing them, and a
    background pixel only lets a character through where the character stands between the tile and what that pixel really
    shows (the floor inside a pillar's tile). "Really shows" is measured from each tile's own nearest part (a tenth of its
    pixels nearer, so a sliver of door frame in a door's tile doesn't count), not per .map Z, with a 15% margin.
  - FIELD_PBR_VIEW 14 shows how far each pixel is behind its tile's nearest part.

Speed
  - The character draws (219 a frame in bvtr_3) each asked the driver about 35 things (blend, depth, uniforms...); with a
    threaded driver every question waits for the driver. FIELD_GL_TRACK keeps a copy of that state from hooks on the calls
    that set it; it checks itself against the driver now and then and goes back to asking if they ever differ (logged).
  - The field files that run every frame are built optimised (the project only has a Debug build); buffers are reused
    instead of made for every draw; the background draws read their vertices in one go.
  - FIELD_PBR_QUALITY sets shadow maps (how many, one or four samples), reflections, and how many steps the checks for
    walls between a surface and an added light take, on the background and on the characters.
  - Lights that add nothing cost nothing per pixel; found lights now start that way (below).
  - Ctrl+F11's frame log says how long DEMASTER's steps took: CPU (average of the frames before) and GPU (that frame).

fieldprep: lights by inverse lighting
  - The old finder took bright spots (lamps' highlights on a shiny floor, signs) for lights and missed lamps whose fixture
    isn't brighter than its pool. Now it fits point lights to the painting's diffuse light (_BL, else the lightmap) with
    your normal and depth maps: the few lights, in view or out of it, whose n.l / r^2 light over the room best explains it
    on top of a smooth ambient (greedy, non-negative least squares, each light moved to where it fits best). Each light's
    comment says how much of the light it explains. --light-share sets how much a light must explain (2%).
  - Found lights start off: intensity 0, shadow 0. Turn on the ones you want. (v15.4 turns them on again.)
  - <field>_LIGHTS.png: your marks on a copy of the field. Pure green dots add a lamp; pure red keeps lamps (and the fit)
    away from that part. Newer than _lights.txt: the lights are found again; the old file stays as <field>_lights_old.txt
    (--relight keeps it too).
  - To get the new lights on a field you've prepared: fieldprep --relight <folder> (your edited _lights.txt is kept as
    _lights_old.txt: copy flicker and follows settings back from it).

Tests
  - tests/field_depth_test.cpp (new, Mesa): the nearest-part pass and the stepping aside, door, pillar and tile order cases.
  - tests/field_pbr_test.cpp: inverse lighting on a box room with two lamps, and the marks.

v15 (demaster.conf 1.5.1a) - PBR for field characters and models
---------------------------------------------------------------
New settings (demaster.conf, after FIELD_PBR_VIEW):
  FIELD_PBR_DEPTH=1        PBR backgrounds hide characters per pixel (from _ND), never nearer than the tile's own Z
  FIELD_PBR_SHADOWS=1      character shadows from the field's lights, contact darkening, room shadows for added lights
                           (replace the game's round shadows; their size and darkness are kept)
  FIELD_PBR_REFLECTIONS=1  characters and 3D models reflected in glossy and metal background surfaces
  FIELD_PBR_CHARACTERS=1   characters lit by the room's light grid, the field's lights and their own maps
  FIELD_AVATARS=0          face pictures of each character, Ctrl+F10 saves them to DEMASTER_EXP\avatars\

Backgrounds

    draws; the game's main.frag is back to what it was plus FIELD_SMOOTH. Copy every file in DEMASTER_EXP\shaders\.
  - Per-pixel depth for PBR tiles (FIELD_PBR_DEPTH): the floor inside a pillar's tile no longer hides characters.
  - Sky: layers that scroll on their own are found while drawing and treated as sky; "sky = 1" in _pbr.txt for a sky
    painted into the foreground's layer.
  - Lights can follow the game's background light groups: "follows = <group>" and "on_states = ..." in _lights.txt
    (lamps switching on, alarm pulses, animated fires). English/European game only for now.
  - "shadow = 0-1" per light: how much a character's shadow takes away from it (painted lights 0.5 by default).
  - Room shadows: walls and pillars block the lights you add (stepping across the whole field's _ND).

Characters and field models
  - DEMASTER collects every model the game draws in a frame and draws them itself right before the background, in the
    game's order and with the game's state: smooth normals over the whole model, lit by the room, the round shadows and
    the game's script entities tell twins apart. 145 draws in bvtr_1 become a handful.
  - The whole model: on PBR fields with shadows, reflections or avatars on, the game's own back-face removal is turned
